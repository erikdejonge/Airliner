# ✈️ Airliner

**Buckle up, captain — the whole world is your runway.**

Airliner is a fast, minimalist geography game for the iPhone. You sit in the
cockpit of a jumbo jet cruising over a gorgeous satellite map of the Earth, and
air-traffic control keeps radioing in your next destination:

> *"Fly to: Madagascar."*
> *"Fly to: Iceland."*
> *"Fly to: Peru."*

The catch? There's no dotted line holding your hand. You have to actually **know
where the place is** — and get there before you run out of time and fuel.

## How to play

- **Tap** anywhere on the map to set your heading. Your airliner banks smoothly
  onto its new course, trailing a satisfying pair of contrails behind it.
- Beat the **fuel gauge** counting down at the edge of the screen
  (green → orange → red). Touch down on target in time and you pocket the
  points, top up the tank, and get your next assignment.
- Miss it and you **lose a life** — the autopilot takes over and flies you
  straight to the spot you couldn't find ("*Time is up: Switzerland*"), so it
  sticks in your memory for next time.
- In a hurry? Punch the **turbo** to trade fuel for extra speed.
- A little **radar minimap** in the corner shows where you are and roughly where
  the target lies — just enough of a hint to keep things fair.

It starts gentle (hello, Australia) and quietly turns you into a walking atlas,
one tiny island nation at a time.

## Two editions

- **Airliner — World Edition** 🌍 — every country on Earth is fair game.
- **Airliner — USA Edition** 🇺🇸 — zoom in on the United States and master all 50
  states (and a surprising number of counties along the way).

## Under the hood

Airliner was originally built back in 2010 with the [cocos2d](https://github.com/cocos2d/cocos2d-objc)
engine for the very first iPhones. It's a compact, old-school Objective-C game:
a hand-rolled 2D engine, satellite imagery, and a physics-free flight model that's
all about feel. This branch revives it to build and run on modern Xcode and iOS —
now rendering crisply at native (retina) resolution — while keeping the classic
gameplay exactly as it was.
